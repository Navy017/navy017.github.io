On Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?

One of the more interesting things in Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep? is definitely how they tell apart the humans from the androids. It is very interesting, though, as something captures slowly what it takes to be a human: as it is able to learn what we say and do, the tests become more and more about the parts of us it cannot yet copy, and hence the tests themselves keep on changing. Changing to what is still hard for the machine to do, where it still cannot fool us, till slowly the gap gets narrower and narrower. Something that isn't truly human yet slowly captures more and more of what being a human is, till it is indistinguishable.

How close do you think one can get to being like a human without being one, huh? Is it truly possible for something to, in all its essence, be like something else without becoming that thing? To capture more and more like another being, is it possible to do so without also, in a sense, becoming the other being?

Just throwing it out there, since I do find it very interesting how a book that old was able to capture an essence of the problem that we face as of now, as the internet gets filled with videos and content generated with AI, and ideas, and slop.

I do think one of the worries we did not have as such with bots, as we do with AI, is specifically that over time it will get closer and closer to real content, and that then it will be a whole lot harder to tell.

Does it matter who made it?

One of the things I have seen when exploring social media is that there is a notion of: even if a model is indistinguishable from humans, we care about the story behind it. And here is why I find that odd.

Sure, to a good bunch of, call it similarity on the surface, it can copy. But then it is very easy to distinguish AI from human art if you look into it; good art especially has a habit of keep on giving the more you look into it. So first of all, I do not believe that it will be that simple for AI to copy human art to begin with, at least not the aspects that make it so cherished, so interesting.

Yet if AI could make a good musical piece, could write a great novel that touches on complex themes and incites emotions, then do you truly not believe that it could be something just as meaningful as human art? That is to say, I personally do not care much about the origin of said piece. Because of course that would mean, for the same piece of work, I will change how I feel about it based on the origin, of which I have no way of seeing. But also because I do believe that the story, the complexity, is captured by the end product itself.
